Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

HIS LIFE

A Life Dedicated to Science and the Nation

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. He studied physics and later specialized in aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. His early interest in science and engineering became the foundation for a remarkable career.

Kalam worked with the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). He played an important role in India's space and missile development programmes. As Project Director of the SLV-III programme, he contributed to the successful launch that placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in 1980.

His work in India's indigenous missile programmes earned him the popular title "Missile Man of India". He also served as Scientific Adviser to the Defence Minister and contributed to national technology and defence programmes. His scientific career was closely connected with India's efforts to develop indigenous technological capabilities.

In 2002, Dr. Kalam became the 11th President of India and served until 2007. During and after his presidency, he remained closely connected with students and education. He wrote several books and continued to encourage young people to pursue knowledge, innovation and national development.
